# ⚙️ Configuration

## 1. Get a token

The City of Warsaw gives out a free access token. Without one the integration cannot read the timetables.

1. Open [dane.um.warszawa.pl/pl/key-api](https://dane.um.warszawa.pl/pl/key-api).
2. Enter a nickname, accept the terms and download your token. It is a long text in three parts separated by dots.

The City also had an **old API**, with keys that look like `xxxxxxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxxxxxxxxxx`. That server stopped answering on 21 October 2026, and the City did not say why. The integration no longer asks it. A line that still has an old key gets no new timetable and says so in its *Status* sensor and in a notice in **Repairs**. To bring it back, replace the old key with a token: see [Change the token](#5-change-the-token-or-replace-an-old-key) below. You cannot set up a new line with an old key.

## 2. Add a line

**Settings → Devices & Services → Add Integration → Warsaw Public Transport.** A few short screens:

1. **The token.** Paste the token. When a line is set up with a token already, this screen is a short menu instead: *Use the token already in use* (it is told by its last four characters and its lines, and you are asked which only when more than one is in use) or *Paste another token*.
2. **The stop.** Type a part of the stop's name, for example `Rondo Daszyńskiego` or `Dworzec Centralny`, or its ID. Capital and Polish letters do not matter, and the City's abbreviations are understood (`dworzec centr` finds *Dw. Centralny*). When the name fits several stops, pick yours.
3. **The pole.** The integration asks this only when the stop has more than one pole with lines. Each pole is listed with where it goes, for example *Rondo Daszyńskiego 01 → Dworzec Centralny*, and its number is on the sign. Pick the direction you travel in. The City's list of stops has no direction, so the integration reads it from the timetable of the pole's first lines.
4. **The line.** Only the lines that stop at that pole today are listed, so there is nothing to mistype. With a token you can also tick *Show the vehicles of this line on the map* here.

You can change the vehicles choice later with **Configure**.

The setup checks the line with the City before it creates anything. You get one entry for the **line** (titled like `Line 151 · Rondo Daszyńskiego 01`) with the stop under it, and a sensor named like `sensor.line_151_from_7009_01`.

Know the numbers? Tick **I know the numbers** on the first screen and type the stop ID, the pole and the line. That form also opens by itself when the City's list of stops cannot be loaded.

## 3. Add another stop to a line

On the entry choose **Add stop** (it asks only for the stop and the pole, and uses the line's token), or add the integration again with the same line: the stop joins the existing entry. A different line is a new entry. Each stop is its own sensor and its own device, and the same stop, pole and line cannot be added twice.

## 4. Walking time to a stop

Open the ⋮ menu of a stop → **Reconfigure** and enter how many minutes you need to walk there (0 to 120, 0 means none).

The sensor then skips departures you could not catch: its state is the first departure you can still reach, and `Next 1` to `Next 3` follow it. It moves on by itself as time passes. The setting is in the attribute `Walking time, Minutes`. The calendar and the City's own timetable are not changed.

## 5. Change the token, or replace an old key

**Configure** on an entry (or **Reconfigure** in the line's menu) replaces its token. The stop and the line stay, and the token is checked with the City before it is saved.

A line with an old key shows one notice in **Settings → Repairs** ("Authentication expired for Line …"), once for the whole line and not once per stop, and a *Reconfigure* button on the integration page. The notice, **Configure** and **Reconfigure** all offer these choices:

| Choice | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Paste a token** | replaces the old key. Tick *Use it for every line that still has an old key* to do all your lines at once |
| **Use the token already entered for another line** | takes a token another line has, without showing it. With more than one token you pick by its last four characters and the lines that use it |
| **Keep the old key and ignore this notice** | keeps things as they are; the notice stays ignored |

## 6. Optional entities

Switch these on in the entity settings of a stop. They are off by default because most people do not need them.

| Entity | What it is |
|---|---|
| `..._next_1`, `_next_2`, `_next_3` | the three departures after the next one, each a time you can use in a card or an automation |
| `calendar.…_timetable` | every departure of the day as a calendar event, so an automation can start "8 minutes before the bus" |
| `sensor.…_last_update` | when the timetable was last fetched |

### The vehicles of a line on the map

Choose **Configure** on the line and tick *Show the vehicles of this line on the map* (the line needs a token). Leave the token field empty to keep the token you have. Every vehicle of the line then appears as one pin on the map, whatever number of stops the line has. The pins of the stop poles are always there.

The vehicle pins are listed on the integration's page under one device per line (named like the line, with the model *Vehicles on the map*), and each has a stable entity ID made from the line and the vehicle's number (`geo_location.line_126_1722`). When the City stops reporting a vehicle, its pin leaves the registry and the device goes with the last one. The pins of the stop poles are shared by the lines that stop there, so they have no registry entry and show only on the map and in the developer tools.

A vehicle pin is named after where it goes, for example *Line 126 → Rechniewskiego*, and carries everything the City says about the vehicle, by where the City says it:

| Attribute | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| `Line`, `Brigade`, `Vehicle number`, `Position time` | the City's positions of the vehicles |
| `Direction`, `Route`, `Notes`, `Scheduled at`, `Scheduled departure` | the timetable of your stops: the departure of the vehicle's brigade that is nearest to now (within 40 minutes) |
| `Operator`, `Depot`, `Registration`, `Year of production`, `Vehicle type` | the City's list of its fleet, read once a day and matched by the vehicle number (a bus and a tram can have the same number, so the line says which) |
| `Make`, `Model`, `Size class`, `Powertrain`, `Air conditioning`, `Floor`, `Seats`, `Standing places`, `Capacity` | the City's description of the vehicle's model, which the fleet list points to |
| `Speed, km/h`, `Course, degrees` | worked out by the integration from the last two positions, when the vehicle has moved at least 25 metres between them |

`Direction` is the **planned** destination of the brigade, not a live one. When a bus gets a shortened course or a diversion, the City's timetable does not say so, and the pin keeps the planned destination. When none of your stops has a departure of the brigade near now, the pin is named with the vehicle's number and has none of the timetable's attributes. The facts from the fleet appear a moment after the vehicles do (the list is big), and are left out for a vehicle the City's fleet does not know.

The state of a vehicle pin is its distance from home, and it changes with every position, so Home Assistant records a new state every half a minute. An integration cannot switch the recording of a state off, but you can exclude the pins in `configuration.yaml`:

```yaml
recorder:
  exclude:
    entity_globs:
      - geo_location.line_*
logbook:
  exclude:
    entity_globs:
      - geo_location.line_*
```


## 7. Remove a stop or a line

- **A stop:** **Settings → Devices & Services → Warsaw Public Transport**, open the ⋮ menu of the stop → **Delete**. Its sensors go with it. If its device stays behind on the integration page, open the device and choose **Delete**.
- **A whole line:** the ⋮ menu of the entry → **Delete**. All its stops and sensors go.
- **The integration:** delete every entry first. If you installed it through Marketplace or HACS, remove it there and restart Home Assistant. If you installed it manually, delete the folder `/config/custom_components/ztm_warsaw` and restart Home Assistant.

## Upgrading from 1.1.x

When 1.2 starts for the first time, the entries of one line become one entry with its stops. Your sensors keep their names, IDs, areas and settings. Two things to know:

- Each stop is now a device of its own, so an automation or card that points at the old **device** needs choosing again. One that points at the **sensor** is not affected.
- There is no way back to 1.1.4, so take a backup first if you want one.
